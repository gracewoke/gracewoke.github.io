Telecom · Customer retention

Where Maven Communications loses customers, and a plan to keep the valuable ones

A California phone and internet provider lost more than a quarter of its customers in one quarter. I took the role of a consultant briefing the Chief Marketing Officer. The brief was to find who leaves and why, predict who leaves next, and show where a retention budget earns the most.

I chose this problem because I worked it from the other side. At British Gas, I handled customers on the point of leaving.

Situation
7,043 subscribers, one quarter of CRM data (Q2 2022).
Complication
1,869 customers churned, taking $1.65M of annual recurring revenue.
Key question
Which current customers should we act on, with what offer, to protect the most revenue per dollar spent?

01 · WHO LEAVES

Churn is a first-year, month-to-month problem

Existing customers only. The 454 who joined this quarter had no chance to leave yet.

Month-to-month customers churn at 51.7%, twenty times the two-year rate (2.6%). Three in four new customers leave within six months.

02 · WHY THEY LEAVE

Competitors win on devices and offers; support attitude is the biggest fixable cause

Every churned customer gave a reason, weighted here by the revenue they took.

Competitors explain 45% of churn and $765K of lost revenue. The top reason Maven controls is support staff attitude. Customers without tech support churn at 44.5% vs 15.5% with it.

03 · WHO IS NEXT

A churn-risk score for every current customer

Two models compared on a 25% held-out test set. Gradient boosting won and scores every current customer's chance of leaving.

744 current customers are high-risk: 91% month-to-month, 74% in their first year. They hold 59% of revenue at risk.

04 · WHAT TO DO

A tiered retention playbook that returns about $1.88 per $1 spent

Risk tier × customer value (monthly charge vs. the $63.20 median), each segment with a play sized to what it could lose.

Assumptions to validate in a pilot: offers keep 30% of high-risk and 20% of medium-risk would-be churners; cost per customer $15 to $120. Savings count one year of revenue.

Fix the first six months

Onboarding check-ins at day 30 and 90, and a 12-month price lock to move customers onto contracts.

Make support a retention tool

Coach front-line staff on save conversations; bundle a tech-support trial for high-risk customers.

Answer the competitor offer

Redesign Offer E (67.6% churn) and pilot device upgrades for the high-value, high-risk segment ($292K at risk).

How I built it

Python (pandas, scikit-learn) with AI-assisted coding. Logistic regression vs. gradient boosting, permutation importance, out-of-fold scoring.
